Let's Be Honest
Questions We Know You're Asking
If you're uncertain, skeptical, or even a little worried about AI — that's completely reasonable. Here are honest answers to the questions we hear most often from performing arts staff.
Will AI replace my job?
This is the most common concern — and it deserves a direct answer. At The Bushnell, AI is a tool, not a replacement. The creative judgment, relationship-building, and artistic sensibility that define this organization's work cannot be automated. What AI can do is handle the repetitive, time-consuming parts of your job — first drafts, formatting, summarizing long documents — so your expertise goes further. Organizations that thrive will be ones where talented people are freed from drudgery, not replaced by it.
✦ Our take: AI amplifies your expertise. It doesn't substitute for it.
Can AI just make things up?
Yes — and this is important to understand. AI tools can produce text that sounds confident and credible but is factually wrong. This is called "hallucination." You should never publish, send, or submit AI-generated content without reviewing it yourself. Specific facts, statistics, donor histories, funder details, and dates are especially vulnerable. Think of AI as a very fast first-draft writer who sometimes invents footnotes — you're the editor of record, always.
✦ Our take: Always verify facts. AI drafts; you decide.
Is my data safe when I use these tools?
It depends on which tool and what you paste into it. Never enter donor names, financial data, personnel records, or confidential contracts into a public AI tool. The Bushnell's AI policy requires that sensitive information be anonymized before use. Claude and Copilot have data protection options suitable for professional use — the Playbook covers which tools are approved and how to use them safely. When in doubt, anonymize first (swap real names for [Name], amounts for [Amount]) and you get the full benefit with no exposure.
✦ Our take: Use approved tools, anonymize sensitive data, follow the policy.
Do I need to be technical to use this?
Not at all. Every tool in this Playbook works through plain English conversation — you type what you need, the same way you'd explain it to a colleague. There is no code, no setup, and no technical expertise required. If you can write an email, you can use AI. The Take 5 section at the top of this page was specifically designed for people who have never used any of these tools before — start there and you'll be productive within the hour.
✦ Our take: If you can write an email, you can use AI. Start with Take 5.
What if I use it wrong or get a bad result?
There's no permanent wrong answer in AI — every session starts fresh. If you get a bad result, try rephrasing your prompt, adding more context, or asking the AI to revise. The worst that happens is you get an unhelpful response and try again. No data is lost, no systems are affected, and no one is monitoring your individual prompts. The Craft section of this Playbook covers the specific techniques that consistently produce better results — but honestly, the fastest way to improve is simply to use it more often.
✦ Our take: You can't break it. Bad results are just prompts to refine.
Isn't using AI cheating, or somehow less authentic?
This is a genuinely interesting question — and one the arts world is actively debating. At The Bushnell, authenticity comes from your values, relationships, and judgment — not from whether a first draft was typed manually. A development officer who uses AI to draft a grant proposal still brings the organizational knowledge, funder relationships, and editorial judgment that make it compelling. Writers have always used tools: dictation, spell-check, research assistants. AI is the latest in that continuum. What you do with the output is still entirely yours.
✦ Our take: Your judgment, values, and voice are what make the work authentic.

◆   Craft Note

Chain Your Prompts for Better Results

Instead of one "do everything" prompt, break complex tasks into a staged sequence. First ask AI to create an outline. Then write each section separately. Finally ask it to unify the tone. This staged approach produces dramatically better results — and gives you creative control at every step.

Give AI a Memory with Context Blocks

At the start of any recurring task, paste a short "context block" — 3–5 sentences about your organization, audience, and tone — before your actual request. AI has no memory between sessions, so this single habit ensures every output reflects The Bushnell's voice without you having to re-explain it each time. Save your context block in a notes app for easy reuse.

Ask AI to Critique Its Own Output

After receiving a draft, follow up with: "Now review this response critically. What's weak, missing, or could be misread by the audience?" This self-critique step consistently surfaces gaps that a first pass misses — especially for donor communications, grant narratives, or anything where nuance matters. Then ask it to revise based on its own critique.

Use Constraints to Sharpen Output

Vague prompts produce vague results. Adding specific constraints — word limits, forbidden phrases, required structure, or a named audience — forces the model to make deliberate choices. Try: "Write this in under 100 words, avoid jargon, do not use the word 'excited,' and end with a question." Constraints are one of the fastest ways to close the gap between AI output and your actual needs.

Anonymize First, Then Analyze

Before pasting any sensitive document — a contract, donor record, personnel file, or financial report — do a quick find-and-replace: swap real names for [Donor A], dollar amounts for [Amount X], and vendor names for [Vendor]. This takes under two minutes and means you get the full analytical power of AI without exposing confidential data. Per The Bushnell's AI policy, this step is required — not optional.

The Craft
How We Work with AI
How to use AI responsibly, skillfully, and in a way that always reflects The Bushnell's standard of care. Each principle includes real prompt examples and Do/Don't comparisons.
01
🎯
Give AI a Role, Context & Goal
The single most impactful improvement you can make: tell the AI who it is, what you need, for whom, and in what format before you ask anything. This transforms generic output into something genuinely useful.
You are a nonprofit arts communications specialist. Write a 3-paragraph donor stewardship email to a major gift prospect, warm but professional.
Write an email to a donor.
Full Performing Arts Prompt Template

"You are a [role]. I need [output type] for [audience/recipient]. The tone should be [adjective]. The goal is [specific outcome]. Here is the context: [background information]."

02
🔐
Protect Confidential Information
Never paste donor names, gift amounts, staff personnel records, financial data, or proprietary contracts into any public AI tool. Always anonymize before asking for help.
Summarize this contract language [paste anonymized version] and flag any unusual renewal clauses.
Here is our touring contract with [show name] for $450,000. Summarize it.
Safe Anonymization Practice

Replace real names with [Donor A], [Vendor], or [Staff Member]. Replace dollar amounts with [Amount X]. The AI will still help you perfectly — and your data stays protected.

03
✅
Always Verify — AI Can Hallucinate
AI models can confidently generate incorrect facts, invented statistics, and fake citations. Before any output goes into a report, press release, or grant, verify key claims from primary sources — especially numbers and dates.
AI drafted says "NEA funding rose 12% in 2024." Cross-check at arts.gov before including in the grant application.
Paste AI-generated funding statistics directly into a grant proposal without verification.
The Verification Habit

After using AI for research: use Perplexity to cross-check any specific statistic or claim. For funding data, always visit the original source (NEA, state arts councils, foundation websites).

04
🔄
Iterate: The First Draft Is Never Final
Treat AI like a collaborator, not a vending machine. Push back, redirect, and build on each response. "Make this shorter," "add more warmth," "write a version for a first-time patron" — each follow-up sharpens the result.
Good start. Now make the opening line more dramatic, remove the second paragraph, and add a specific reference to our PARTNERS program.
Accept the first response if it doesn't quite feel right and move on.
Refinement Prompts to Try

"Rewrite this in a warmer, more personal tone." / "Make this 30% shorter without losing the key message." / "Write 3 different versions of the opening line — range from formal to conversational."

05
📐
Specify Format, Length & Audience
AI will default to a generic format if you don't specify. Tell it exactly what you want: the number of paragraphs, whether to use bullet points, who will read it, and what action you want them to take.
Write a 2-paragraph summary for our board of trustees — assume they know The Bushnell but not the details of this program. End with a clear recommendation.
Summarize this for our board. [No additional context provided]
Format Specification Examples

"Write as a single paragraph, no bullet points." / "Give me exactly 3 options." / "Keep it under 150 words." / "Format this as a table with columns for [X] and [Y]."

06
👤
Always Add the Human Touch
AI is your starting point, never your finish line. Every donor letter, press release, and grant narrative needs you — your knowledge of the relationship, your organizational culture, your voice. Donors and partners can sense when something lacks genuine human care.
Use AI for structure and first draft, then add: the patron's specific history, a personal observation, The Bushnell's voice, and our mission connection.
Send AI output directly without review, personalization, or applying your own professional judgment.
07
🎭
Apply Bushnell Voice & Culture to All Output
AI doesn't know The Bushnell's history, values, or relationships. You must bring that to everything it produces. Before finalizing any AI-assisted content, ask: Does this sound like us? Does it reflect our mission to elevate the spirit? Does it honor the community we serve?
Infusing Bushnell's Voice

"After generating a draft, add context: Our organization's mission is to elevate the spirit through the performing arts. Our tone is warm, civic-minded, and deeply connected to Hartford. Does this draft reflect those values? If not, revise it."

08
📋
Build a Shared Prompt Library
Save prompts that produce great results. Create a shared team document of high-performing prompts for recurring tasks — donor letters, press releases, grant summaries, season announcements, meeting recap templates. This builds institutional AI knowledge that grows over time and helps everyone start from a stronger baseline.
What to Save in Your Library

Category: Donor Communications. Prompt: "You are a major gifts officer at a nonprofit performing arts center. Write a [type] letter to a donor who has given for [X] years..."

Keep a running Google Doc or Word file organized by task type. Include the prompt, the tool used, and a note on what made it effective.

09
⛓️
Chain Prompts for Complex Tasks
For multi-part projects, break the task into a sequence of smaller prompts rather than one giant request. This gives you more control, better quality at each step, and the ability to course-correct before moving forward.
Grant Writing Chain Example

Step 1: "Create an outline for a program narrative for [program]." → Step 2: "Now write Section 1 — the community need statement — in 150 words." → Step 3: "Review this full draft and flag any inconsistencies in tone or any claims that need a data source."

10
📚
Use Role-Setting System Prompts
Open any conversation by telling the AI who it is and what expertise to draw from. This single step dramatically improves relevance and quality across the entire conversation — not just the first response.
Performing Arts Role Prompts

"You are an experienced nonprofit arts development professional..." / "You are a performing arts marketing director at a regional theater..." / "You are an audience development specialist who understands the Hartford, CT community..."

11
⚖️
Know Copyright & Disclosure Requirements
AI-generated content can sometimes reflect its training data. For important published content, check for originality. Know your organization's policy on disclosing AI use in external-facing materials, and be aware that grant funders may have their own requirements on this topic.
12
🌱
Keep Learning — AI Evolves Fast
Features that didn't exist six months ago may transform how you work today. Dedicate 20–30 minutes monthly to exploring new capabilities and share discoveries with your team. Innovation at The Bushnell starts with curious, engaged people — just like you.
Monthly Learning Habit

"What new features have been released for [Claude / ChatGPT / Copilot] in the last 3 months? Give me a 3-bullet summary of the most useful ones for a nonprofit arts organization."

13
🚨
Know When NOT to Use AI
AI is powerful — but there are moments when it must step aside entirely. Crisis communications, sensitive personnel matters, and deeply personal donor situations require authentic human voice and judgment from someone who knows The Bushnell's relationships and values.
A patron complaint about an accessibility issue: respond personally and with full knowledge of the situation. AI drafts a starting point, but you own the relationship.
Use AI to draft a public response during a PR crisis or community controversy without full leadership review and substantial human revision.
14
🤝
Use AI to Prepare, Not to Replace
Some of AI's highest value is in helping you show up better — for a donor meeting, board presentation, or funder call — not in replacing the conversation itself. Use it to anticipate questions, sharpen talking points, and build your confidence.
Pre-Meeting Prep Prompt

"I have a cultivation meeting with a prospective major donor focused on arts education. They've attended 3 events but never made a gift. Generate 5 questions they might ask me, 3 possible objections, and my strongest responses to each."

15
📅
Know Your Tool's Knowledge Cutoff
Most AI models have a training cutoff date — they may not know about recent show closings, funding policy changes, or current events. For anything time-sensitive, always cross-check with a live-search tool like Perplexity or Gemini.
Use Perplexity or Gemini to verify any claim about current funder priorities, recent arts industry data, or newly announced programs before citing them.
Assume Claude or ChatGPT knows about a Broadway show that closed recently, a new NEA program announced this year, or recent leadership changes at a foundation.

House Rules
The Bushnell's AI Policy
The Bushnell's official policy on Artificial Intelligence — covering approved tools, the approval process, data privacy requirements, and your responsibilities as a staff member. All employees are required to observe these guidelines.
◆   Official Policy Statement

The Bushnell recognizes the growing use of Artificial Intelligence (AI), including Generative AI tools, in the workplace. AI tools have the potential to automate tasks, improve decision-making, and provide valuable insights into our operations. While AI can enhance efficiency and productivity, its use must align with The Bushnell's security, confidentiality, and compliance standards. The Bushnell has established an AI Committee that is responsible for overseeing and administering this policy.

🔑   GenAI Technology Approval Process

The Bushnell may pilot or fully deploy GenAI tools that are licensed for use (e.g. Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT). If you are interested in using a new AI tool not already approved, you must obtain approval before using, purchasing, subscribing to, or installing any generative AI technology. All software purchases and subscriptions are managed through the Information Technology department.

01
Submit a Proposal
Submit a detailed proposal to the AI Committee outlining the intended use, benefits, and potential risks of the generative AI application.
02
Committee Assessment
The AI Committee evaluates the ethical and legal implications of the proposed application. Additional members may be assigned depending on the nature of the proposal.
03
Approval & Deployment
Only upon receiving AI Committee approval can the generative AI application be installed, deployed, or subscribed to. IT manages all purchases.
The AI Committee includes senior leadership from various departments and is determined on a case-by-case basis. Questions about the approval process should be directed to your department head or the IT department.
🧠
Human Judgment Always Applies
AI should not be used as a substitute for human judgment. Rely on human expertise to evaluate what AI creates. As with any knowledge tool, assume it can and will make mistakes. Employees are responsible for verifying the accuracy and reliability of AI-generated outputs before using them in business decisions or communications.
● Your responsibility to verify
🔐
Protection of Confidential Information
Maintain the highest level of privacy practices with all information exchanged with an AI tool. Do not upload or share any data that is confidential, proprietary, or protected by regulation. This includes data related to customers, employees, or other individuals with which The Bushnell does business.
● Strictly prohibited
👤
Data Privacy & PII
AI tools must not publicly disclose, store, or share in an unauthorized manner the Personally Identifiable Information (PII) of The Bushnell's employees, customers, patrons, donors, or vendors. All data privacy laws must be followed. Consult management if you have any questions.
● PII strictly off-limits
⚖️
Legal Compliance
AI does not absolve you of your compliance obligations. All laws that govern employment still apply when you use AI — including all decisions relating to hiring and promotions. Do not assume an AI model or its output complies with federal and state laws. Always apply your own legal and ethical judgment.
● Legal obligations remain yours
🛡️
Security Best Practices
Employees must apply the same security best practices used for all company and customer data when using AI tools. This includes using strong passwords, keeping software up-to-date, and following The Bushnell's data retention and disposal policies. Treat AI tools with the same security discipline as any other business system.
● Standard security rules apply
🚫
Compliance with All Company Policies
Employees must use generative AI in accordance with all of The Bushnell's policies — including anti-harassment, anti-discrimination, equal employment opportunity, and all other handbook policies. AI must not be used to create content that is inappropriate, discriminatory, or otherwise harmful to others or to The Bushnell.
● No exceptions
🔍
Fact-Check Everything
Set aside time to fact-check information and materials created by an AI tool. Monitor AI use for discriminatory outcomes and other unlawful practices. AI-generated content — including statistics, citations, names, and dates — must be verified against primary sources before appearing in any official document or communication.
● Verify before publishing
❓
Questions & the AI Committee
The Bushnell's AI Committee is responsible for overseeing this policy, reviewing new tool proposals, and providing guidance on appropriate AI use. If you're unsure whether a particular use is appropriate — ask before you act. Contact your department head or the IT department with questions.
● AI Committee here to help
Policy Definitions
Generative AI (GenAI)
Artificial intelligence systems capable of creating content such as text, images, audio, and video. These systems include, but are not limited to, language models, image synthesis systems, and other content creation algorithms.
Ethical Use
Employing AI technologies in ways that are fair, transparent, and free from bias. It also includes ensuring that the use of AI-generated material does not cause harm and respects privacy and consent.
The Glossary
Words Worth Knowing
Plain-language definitions for the terms you'll encounter as you start using AI tools. No jargon — just clear, practical explanations.
Prompt
The instruction or question you type to an AI tool. A better-written prompt almost always produces a better result. Think of it like a job brief: the clearer and more specific you are, the better the output.
Example: "Write a 2-paragraph donor thank-you in a warm, mission-focused tone" is a much better prompt than "write a donor email."
Hallucination
When an AI confidently generates incorrect, invented, or fictional information — including fake statistics, non-existent citations, or wrong dates. It happens even with the best models. Always verify facts before using them in official documents.
Example: Claude might write "According to the 2024 NEA report, attendance rose 18%" — but that report or figure may not exist.
Context Window
The maximum amount of text an AI can "hold in mind" during a single conversation. Larger context windows mean the AI can work with longer documents. Gemini's 1M+ token window can process entire books; earlier models could only handle a few pages.
Example: If you paste a 50-page grant report and ask Claude to summarize it, its context window determines whether it can "read" the whole thing at once.
Token
The unit AI models use to measure text — roughly 3–4 characters, or about ¾ of a word. Context windows and pricing are measured in tokens. In practice, you don't need to count tokens; just know that longer documents use more of the model's "memory."
Example: "The Bushnell" = approximately 4 tokens. A 10-page document = roughly 5,000–7,000 tokens.
System Prompt
An instruction given to an AI at the start of a conversation that shapes how it behaves throughout. When you open a session by saying "You are a nonprofit development director...", that's acting like a system prompt — it primes the AI's role and tone for everything that follows.
Example: "You are a warm, mission-driven arts communications professional writing for Hartford audiences."
Knowledge Cutoff
The date after which an AI model has no training data. Events, policy changes, or new publications after this date are unknown to the model unless it has real-time web search (like Perplexity or Gemini). Most models have cutoffs 6–18 months before their release.
Example: If a foundation changed its priorities in January 2025 and Claude's cutoff is mid-2024, it won't know about that change.
Prompt Chaining
Breaking a complex task into a sequence of smaller, focused prompts — each building on the last. This produces much better results than one giant "do everything" request and gives you control at every step.
Example: Prompt 1: "Outline a grant narrative." → Prompt 2: "Write Section 1 based on this outline." → Prompt 3: "Review the full draft for tone consistency."
Multimodal
An AI that can process multiple types of input — not just text, but also images, audio, video, and documents. Gemini is highly multimodal. Claude and ChatGPT can both analyze images. This means you can show AI a photo and ask questions about it.
Example: Upload a photo of a gala setup and ask "What improvements to the layout would improve donor flow through the room?"
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
A technique where an AI searches a specific set of documents before answering — so its responses are grounded in your actual content, not just its general training. NotebookLM uses this approach: it only cites from the files you uploaded.
Example: Upload your last 5 annual reports to NotebookLM and ask "How has PARTNERS enrollment changed over time?" — it answers from your actual data.
LLM (Large Language Model)
The underlying technology behind most AI tools. An LLM is trained on vast amounts of text and learns to predict and generate language. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok are all powered by different LLMs — which is why they have different strengths and personalities.
Example: "I used Claude's LLM to draft the grant narrative" just means you used Claude to write it.
Fine-Tuning
Training an AI model on a specific dataset to make it more expert in a particular domain. Some organizations fine-tune AI on their own documents to create a custom assistant. For most Bushnell uses, this level of customization isn't needed — good prompting achieves most goals.
Example: A hospital might fine-tune a model on medical records. For now, The Bushnell achieves similar results through detailed role-setting prompts.
Guardrails
Built-in safety rules that prevent an AI from producing harmful, false, or inappropriate content. All major tools have guardrails, though they differ in strictness. They're why AI tools sometimes decline certain requests — not a bug, but a feature designed to keep outputs safe and responsible.
Example: Claude won't fabricate a quote attributed to a real person, even if asked — that's a guardrail in action.
Temperature
A setting that controls how creative or predictable AI responses are. Low temperature produces consistent, precise output — better for data tasks, summaries, and policy language. High temperature produces more varied, unexpected output — better for brainstorming and creative writing. Most consumer tools manage this automatically, but it explains why the same prompt can produce different results each time you run it.
Example: Asking AI to "generate 10 gala theme ideas" benefits from high temperature. Asking it to "summarize this contract accurately" does not.
Zero-Shot vs. Few-Shot Prompting
Zero-shot means asking AI to complete a task with no examples — just instructions. Few-shot means including 1–3 examples of the output you want before making your request. Few-shot almost always produces better results for formatting-sensitive tasks, because the AI can match the pattern of your examples rather than guessing what you want.
Example (few-shot): "Here is a donor thank-you we love: [example]. Now write a similar one for a first-time patron who attended our gala."
Iteration
The practice of refining AI output through a series of follow-up prompts rather than accepting the first response. Each follow-up — "make this shorter," "adjust the tone," "add a specific reference to our PARTNERS program" — sharpens the result. Iteration is the single most impactful habit to build when working with AI.
Example: Prompt 1 gets a solid draft. Prompt 2 adjusts the opening. Prompt 3 adds mission language. Prompt 4 trims to 150 words. The final output reflects your judgment at every step.
AI Agent / Agentic AI
An AI that can take sequences of actions autonomously — browsing the web, writing and running code, managing files, or completing multi-step tasks — rather than simply responding to a single question. Tools like Microsoft Copilot's automation features and Claude Code are moving in this direction. Relevant as The Bushnell's AI use evolves beyond single-prompt tasks.
Example: An AI agent could be asked to "research three foundations, draft a paragraph on each, and compile them into a formatted briefing document" — completing all steps without further prompting.
Grounding
When an AI ties its responses to specific, verified source material rather than drawing solely from its general training data. A grounded AI is less likely to hallucinate because its answers are anchored to real documents or live data. NotebookLM is grounded in your uploaded files; Perplexity is grounded in live web results.
Example: Ask NotebookLM a question about your grant history and it will cite the exact passage from your uploaded annual report — that's grounding in action.
Model
The specific version of an AI system being used. Different models from the same company have different capabilities, speeds, and costs — for example, Claude Sonnet is faster and lighter, while Claude Opus is more capable for complex reasoning. You may encounter model selectors in tools like ChatGPT or Claude. When in doubt, the default model is usually the best balance of quality and speed for everyday tasks.
Example: ChatGPT offers GPT-4o and GPT-4o mini. For a quick email draft, mini is fine. For a complex grant narrative, the full model produces better results.
Inference
The process that runs when you submit a prompt — the AI model reads your input and generates a response. Understanding inference helps explain why responses take a moment to appear, why longer prompts or documents can be slower to process, and why running the same prompt twice may produce slightly different results each time.
Example: When you paste a 20-page grant report and ask Claude to summarize it, the inference process reads every token of that document before generating a response — which is why it takes a few seconds longer than a short prompt.
Data Residency & Retention
Where your data is stored and for how long after you submit it to an AI tool. This varies significantly by tool and subscription tier. Free plans may store and use your inputs to improve the model; enterprise plans often offer stronger protections including no data retention. This is directly relevant to The Bushnell's AI policy — always check a tool's data practices before submitting any sensitive information.
Example: A free ChatGPT account may use your conversations for training. A Microsoft 365 Copilot subscription under an enterprise agreement typically does not — your data stays within your organization's tenant.
