Step 1

Rules of the Road

Do this

  • —Start with a role prompt — who you are, where you work, what tone you need.
  • —Anonymize before pasting — replace names, amounts, show titles with placeholders.
  • —Review before you send — AI is a first draft, not a final.
  • —Iterate — "shorter," "more warmth," "try again."
  • —Share good prompts — what works for you works for your team.
  • —Only use Claude, ChatGPT, and Microsoft Copilot — any other AI tool needs AI Task Force approval first.

Don't do this

  • —No Personally Identifiable Information (PII) — ever. No real names, addresses, emails, phone numbers, or ID numbers for any donor, patron, employee, or vendor.
  • —No financial information — ever. No gift amounts, salaries, bank details, or account numbers.
  • —Verify facts, stats, and dates — AI hallucinates confidently.
  • —No grant content without expert review — Development Director signs off.
  • —No board, legal, or strategic documents — keep these offline.
  • —Don't use a personal or public ChatGPT or Claude account for work — always sign in through your Bushnell-managed workspace with Bushnell SSO.

Steps 5–7

Try It Safely

Open your approved tool of choice through your Bushnell-managed workspace — Claude and ChatGPT via Bushnell SSO, Copilot through your Bushnell Microsoft 365 account — and try this. Every detail below is a fictional example, not real Bushnell information.

Step 5 — A safe fictional prompt

"You are a communications assistant at a nonprofit performing arts center. Draft a short, warm thank-you note to a volunteer named 'Jordan Rivera' — a made-up name for this example — who helped backstage during a recent gala."

FICTIONAL EXAMPLE ONLY — "Jordan Rivera" is not a real person. Never substitute a real donor, volunteer, or staff name here.

Step 6 — Review it critically. Does the tone sound right? Are any facts, names, or numbers accurate? AI can sound confident and still be wrong.
Step 7 — Revise once. Ask the tool to adjust — "make it warmer" or "shorten to two sentences." One round of revision usually gets you most of the way there.

Key terms

Glossary

Prompt
The instruction you type — the clearer and more specific, the better the result.
Role prompt
Telling the AI who it should act as before asking it to do something.
Hallucination
A confident but incorrect answer. Always verify facts, stats, and dates.
Context window
How much text the AI can "remember" in one conversation.
Fine-tuning
Training a model further on specific data — not something you'll do day-to-day.
Anonymize
Replacing real names, amounts, or identifiers with placeholders before pasting.
Questions

FAQ

Is my data safe if I paste it into these tools?

Only if you're signed in with Bushnell SSO to your Bushnell-managed Claude or ChatGPT workspace — never a personal or public account, which offers none of the same protection. Even then, the safest rule is still: anonymize donor, HR, and financial specifics before pasting anything.

Do I need to disclose that I used AI?

For internal drafts, no. For public-facing content or grant submissions, check with your director — some funders want disclosure.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

It will, occasionally, and confidently. Always verify names, numbers, and dates before anything goes out.

Which tool should I start with?

If you write often, start with Claude. If you work mostly in Outlook/Word/Teams, start with Copilot — it's already there.
