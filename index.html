04✓
Learn the Golden Rule: Don't Share Private Data
The most important habit in AI use. Understand what's safe to share — and what isn't.
All Tools
›

AI tools are incredibly useful — but they're external platforms. Information you type in can be used to train future models or be reviewed by the company's safety team. The rule is simple:

  • ✓ Safe: General questions, anonymized examples, public information, draft writing, brainstorming
  • ✗ Never: Individual donor names + dollar amounts, employee salary/HR data, patron personal data, confidential board materials, vendor contract specifics

When in doubt, ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if this appeared in a Google search?" If no — don't type it. Generalize or anonymize first.

01✓
Master the Art of the Prompt
Learn the CRAFT framework for writing prompts that consistently get excellent results.
All Tools
›

Great prompts share a structure. Use CRAFT to build yours:

  • C — Context: Who you are, your org, the situation
  • R — Role: What role you want the AI to play ("Act as a fundraising expert...")
  • A — Action: The specific thing you need ("Write / Summarize / Analyze / Draft...")
  • F — Format: How you want it ("3 bullet points / a 2-paragraph email / a table...")
  • T — Tone: Voice and register ("Warm and professional / Formal / Concise...")
CRAFT In Action

"Context: I work in development at a nonprofit arts center. Role: Act as an experienced major gifts fundraiser. Action: Draft a re-engagement email for a lapsed donor who gave $5,000 three years ago but hasn't responded to our last two appeals. Format: 3 short paragraphs with a clear subject line. Tone: Warm, personal, no pressure — focus on reconnection."

Rules of the Road

AI tools are powerful — and that means knowing the guardrails matters as much as knowing the shortcuts. These aren't bureaucratic rules; they protect our patrons, donors, and colleagues.

✓ Do This
✓
Start with a role prompt. Tell the AI who you are, what The Bushnell is, and what tone you need before asking it to write anything.
✓
Anonymize all data. Replace patron names with IDs, show names with [Show A], dollar amounts with [Amount X] before pasting anything into an AI tool.
✓
Always review AI output before using it. AI is a first-draft generator, not a final product. Read everything and edit to match your voice and the actual facts.
✓
Use it for the routine so you can focus on the exceptional. Let AI handle the first pass so your expertise can go into the judgment, nuance, and strategy that only you can provide.
✓
Ask follow-up questions. If the first response isn't right, say so. "Make it shorter," "Add more warmth," "Use a different example." AI conversation is iterative.
✓
Share what's working with your team. Good prompts are reusable. If you find something that works, document it and share it.
✗ Don't Do This
✗
Don't paste real donor names + giving amounts. Ever. This is private data that belongs to our patrons — treat it with the same care you would any confidential record.
✗
Don't share employee salaries, HR situations, or performance details. These are confidential personnel matters. Generalize to role types, never to specific individuals.
✗
Don't treat AI output as fact. AI can "hallucinate" — generating confident-sounding information that is simply wrong. Always verify specific facts, statistics, and dates.
✗
Don't submit AI-generated grant content without expert review. Have your Development Director or relevant department lead review any AI-assisted grant materials before submitting.
✗
Don't share confidential board or legal materials. Strategic plans, board executive session content, vendor contracts, and legal correspondence should not be entered into any external AI tool.
✗
Don't forget to read performer/union rights requirements. For content featuring artists or performers, confirm usage rights align with any union or contract obligations before publishing.
Step 4
Key Terms — Plain English

AI has a lot of jargon. Here's what the terms you'll actually encounter actually mean.

AI (Artificial Intelligence)
Computer systems trained on enormous amounts of text and data to understand and generate human language. The tools in this guide are all AI-powered assistants.
LLM (Large Language Model)
The underlying technology behind Claude, ChatGPT, and Copilot. A system trained on vast amounts of text to predict and generate language. When someone says "LLM," they mean the AI engine itself.
Prompt
The instruction or question you type to the AI. Better prompts = better results. Think of it like giving clear directions to a very capable new colleague who knows nothing about your specific situation yet.
Hallucination
When an AI confidently states something that is factually wrong. AI can invent statistics, misattribute quotes, or get dates wrong — with total confidence. Always verify specific facts before using them.
Context Window
How much information the AI can "hold in mind" during a single conversation. Modern AI tools have large context windows — Claude can process very long documents in one go. When you start a new chat, it forgets the previous one.
Role Prompt
A prompt that tells the AI what role to play and what context to assume. Example: "You are a communications professional at a nonprofit arts center." This shapes everything that follows in the conversation.
Iteration
Refining the AI's output through follow-up instructions. "Make it shorter." "Add more warmth." "Change the opening." The best results come from going back and forth, not from expecting perfection on the first try.
Copilot
Microsoft's AI assistant, built into Office 365 apps (Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel, PowerPoint). Unlike standalone AI tools, Copilot works inside the applications you already use every day.
Generative AI
AI that creates new content — text, images, code — rather than just finding or organizing existing information. Claude, ChatGPT, and Copilot are all generative AI tools.
Anonymize
Replacing identifying information with generic placeholders before sharing data with an AI tool. Example: "John Smith" becomes "[Donor]"; "$25,000" becomes "[Major Gift Amount]." A critical privacy habit.
Token
The unit AI uses to measure text length. Roughly 4 characters or ¾ of a word. Not something you usually need to think about — but relevant when processing very long documents (you may hit limits).
Asana Intelligence
Asana's built-in AI feature set. Generates project status reports, surfaces at-risk tasks, drafts updates, and answers questions about your Asana data — without leaving your project management tool.
